Modern Conveniences in Pop Culture from the Decade Immediately After WWII

Yes, we actually made a website.

By Oak, Varvara, Jasper, and Kelsey
Modern conveniences in pop culture are things that have been popular in culture and are relevant to a gain in convenience. We are talking about things that were specifically popular in 1945–1955. ELECTRICAL APPLIANCES
Post WW2, electrical appliances became extremely popular. Things like cooking, doing laundry, hygiene, vacuum cleaners and much more were revolutionised. Labour-saving devices helped people do things faster around their house and keep everything simple.
KITCHEN APPLIANCES
Kitchen appliances were small to emphasise practical use of space, as well as the war’s housing constraints. Electrical appliances were limited, with simple refrigerators, gas or coal stoves, and sinks.
HYGIENE
Laundry machines and dryers became super popular because they made washing clothes and bedding easier, which allowed people to do it more often.
ELECTRONIC DEVICES
Companies broadcasted many things on radios, which were extremely popular. Television sets were already being sold.
BALLPOINT PENS
Ballpoint pens were invented in 1888, the modern version patented in 1938, but they only gained popularity in the late 1940s and early 1950s. This new pen that didn’t have to be dipped in ink quickly replaced steel-nibbed pens.
FUN FACT
When Gimbels department store in New York first sold ballpoint pens in 1945, they cost $12.50 each - over $180 today. They sold 10,000 pens on the first day.
POST‑WAR TIMELINE (1945–1955)
1945 — WWII ends. Consumer factories stop making military gear and start building household appliances.
1947 — Mass production of home appliances begins to skyrocket across suburbs.
1950 — Television sets become the ultimate must-have electronic item in living rooms.
1953 — Ballpoint pens officially take over schools and offices, replacing fountain pens.
